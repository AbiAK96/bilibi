Feedback and states

Short-lived messages, inline guidance, and the empty, blocked and interrupted states. These states are part of the product, not exceptions to its design. Validation, disabled and in-flight states are shown with the controls that have them, under Inputs and Actions.

One message per severity. The layout's flash group is the single production outlet: it stacks messages in one column at the top right, most severe first, so several stay readable at once. Info, warning and error stay until dismissed, because a message someone must act on must not disappear on a timer. Only a success times out, after eight seconds: every completed write reports one, and info is for a message that informs without confirming a write. Each kind has a colour and an icon of its own, so the four can be told apart here side by side; info is blue, as in Belimbing, and never reads as a success. Success and info are announced politely as a status; warning and error interrupt as an alert.

Deliberately outside this contract: the shell's preference status line under the top bar. It reports the in-flight and settled state of the clock and theme controls in place, written by the shell's own client hook before the server answers, so "Saving…" can show at once and a failure stays beside the control it belongs to. A flash is a server fact rendered after the round trip and cannot do that, so the status line is not folded into this outlet and this outlet does not report preference saves.

The outcome of one in-place commit, reported beside the fact that made it rather than in a flash. Every write that saves by itself — an inline edit, a choice that commits on change, a group with one Apply — speaks in this one voice: "Saved" for the most recent commit, and a refusal that names the rejected value and stays on its fact until that fact is committed again.

What the last action on a panel did, reported above the panel's table or inside its open dialog: a LiveComponent cannot reach the page's flash, and a message raised behind an open dialog could be neither read nor dismissed. The kinds follow the flash's rule for what a message does, not how it is worded: a completed write says success and reads as the success flash at inline strength, a plain statement is info and reads as the info flash, and a refusal is an error that interrupts. Dismiss clears it.
